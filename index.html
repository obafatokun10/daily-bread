About the plan

Robert Murray M'Cheyne, a minister in Dundee, drew up this calendar in 1842 for his congregation. Each day has four chapters from different parts of the Bible. Over 365 days you read the Old Testament once and the New Testament and Psalms twice.

He marked the first two readings for Family worship, read together at home, and the last two as Secret, read alone with God. Reading four streams side by side lets one part of Scripture shed light on another.

A way to study each reading

  1. Pray briefly before you start. Ask God to open your eyes to what is there.
  2. Read the passage slowly, all the way through.
  3. Open the commentary. Matthew Henry's notes follow the passage section by section.
  4. Answer the study questions in the journal box for that reading. Writing slows you down and makes you think.
  5. Finish by writing a short prayer from what you read.

If you miss a day, don't try to read three days at once to catch up. M'Cheyne himself warned against reading becoming a burden.

Catch me up

When you fall behind, a Catch me up button appears on Today and in the Calendar. It makes your first unfinished day today's reading and moves every later day back by the same number of days. Nothing is skipped: you still read all 365 days in order, and the plan finishes later. Your ticks and notes stay with their readings, and your streak starts again from that day.

Sources

Reading plan: Robert Murray M'Cheyne, Calendar for Daily Readings (1842). Commentary: Matthew Henry's Concise Commentary on the Whole Bible (public domain). This edition of the commentary has no notes on Leviticus 19 or Psalm 108.

Translations: the King James Version, plus 22 more public-domain translations you can switch between in a reading's Scripture tab. Partial Bibles fall back to the KJV for chapters they do not cover. The NIV, ESV, NKJV, NLT, CSB and NASB are copyrighted, so the app links to them on Bible Gateway instead.

Put it on your Home Screen

iPhone: open this page in Safari, tap the Share button, then Add to Home Screen.
Android: open it in Chrome, tap the ⋮ menu, then Add to Home screen or Install app.

Once installed it opens full screen like an app. Days you have opened are kept on the phone, so you can read them without a connection.
